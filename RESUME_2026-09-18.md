## Authoritative design decisions (from discussion, 2026-09-18)

1. Reserved keys `[0,1,10,15]` stay exclusive to the lemma-homophone `*`/`#` track (unchanged,
   per ROADMAP 2026-09-15). Same-lemma conjugation atoms get **keypresses** (each = 1+ physical
   keys), expected to be realized from the **coda** bank.
2. **Not** "1 keypress = 1 atomic feature". One keypress may represent **several atomic features
   that never compete** — e.g. `subjonctif`/`conditionnel`/`infinitif` can share one keypress if
   no cluster ever needs them to discriminate against each other. A word needing several atoms
   presses the **union (combo)** of their keypresses — e.g. subjonctif-keypress + nbr_p-keypress
   vs subjonctif-keypress + nbr_s-keypress. (This is exactly what dissolves the review's
   infeasibility finding, which was aimed at §3's literal text: "no two atoms' assigned key-sets
   may share a physical key, ever" caps assignable atoms at the 10 coda keys vs a real inventory
   of ~20 — that constraint text is wrong and must go.)
3. **Scope cut**: physical assignment (which coda keys realize each keypress, collision-feasibility
   vs the live theory, biomechanical cost, two-stroke fallback, `dictionary.py` wiring and
   persistence) is **out of scope for now**. In scope: determine the **number of keypresses
   needed (K)** and **which atomic feature(s) each keypress represents** — the abstract layer.

## The corrected model: union-injectivity

Pairwise "never compete" is *necessary but not sufficient* — collisions can arise from unions
coinciding with no shared keypress: if `a`,`b` share keypress P1 and `c`,`d` share P2 (both pairs
individually non-competing), a word needing `{a,c}` and a rival needing `{b,d}` both press
`{P1,P2}`. Conversely, union-level distinctness can *permit* sharing a pairwise rule would
forbid (in a parle/parles/parlent cluster, `pers_2` and `nbr_p` may share a keypress because the
unions `{K_p2}` vs `{K_p3, K_np}` still differ).

So the hard constraint is the ground truth itself: **within each homophone cluster, the map
member → set-of-keypresses (union of its atoms' keypresses; canonical member = ∅) must be
injective.** "Share when they never compete" becomes an emergent outcome the solver discovers,
not an input rule it trusts. Concrete shape:

- Decision vars: one of K abstract keypresses (or ∅) per atom, exactly-one — the `_colorFeatures`
  schema (`src/satoptimizer.py:155-259`), K found via the same feasibility loop as
  `_minSpecialKeypressesNeeded` (`:262-279`).
- Plus one constraint per unordered pair of cluster members: their keypress-SETS differ
  (XOR-style encoding over per-word "uses keypress p" booleans). ~20 atoms, a few tens of
  thousands of pair constraints — trivial for CP-SAT.
- The pairwise compete/co-occur graph is still worth computing for reporting and as a coloring
  lower bound on K, but not as the constraint itself.

### Rules proposed this session, NOT yet confirmed by the user — confirm first

- **No sharing among co-occurring atoms** (e.g. `subjonctif` and `présent` co-occur in
  `subjonctif:présent:pers_3:nbr_s`): sharing there would compress chords but erode the
  compositional semantics the whole plan exists for; taken to the limit it degenerates into
  per-word unique keypresses. Recommended edge rule: share only if atoms *neither compete nor
  co-occur anywhere*.
- **Canonical-member choice as a per-cluster decision variable** instead of
  `_selectCanonicalIndex`'s pre-solve fixed pick (`src/ambiguitychecker.py:191-199`) — it directly
  changes minimum K and the objective, and is the same genre of pre-solved decision the plan
  criticizes elsewhere.
- **Objective = minimize K + a frequency-weighted chord-size term.** With the chord-size term,
  "free-rider" atoms (present in every member of a cluster, e.g. `indicatif`/`présent` in the
  parler family, discriminating nothing) automatically get ∅ — reproducing "no keypress =
  unmarked default" mechanically, with no `FEATURE_PRIORITY` table.

## Review findings deferred to the physical phase (keep, don't lose)

- **Cross-cluster new-vs-new collisions**: feasibility vs the static `theory`
  (`_isFeasibleAddition`, `src/ambiguitychecker.py:243`) misses collisions between two *newly
  composed* chords — e.g. final stroke `(12,16)` + addition `{18}` and `(12,18)` + `{16}` both
  land on `(12,16,18)`. Also: the `*`/`#` reserved-key modifications create strokes absent from
  `theory` too; the composition order of the two tracks is undefined and interacts.
- **Physical pressability filtering**: `getStrokeCost` (`src/keyboard.py:529-546`) does per-finger
  dict lookups that KeyError on illegal unions; coda `m`=(25,) + `n`=(22,) gives right pinky
  {22,25}, not in `_possibleKeypress.rightPinky` (`src/keyboard.py:343-348`). Evidence the
  current diagnostic already emits impossible candidates: `anchor_feasibility.tsv` lists `m+n` as
  feasible. Also `checkComposedChords:336` takes `feasibleComboPhonemes[0][0]` — half of a
  2-phoneme combo.
- **Cost object** = the full merged final stroke (base ∪ additions), not the added keys;
  two-stroke fallback collisions live in stroke-SEQUENCE space (`theory` keys are `Strokes`
  tuples, multi-stroke entries already exist).
- **Mechanical invariants** for the eventual §8-analog: stroke→word injectivity from persisted
  output; one keyset per atom; every delivered stroke per-finger pressable.
