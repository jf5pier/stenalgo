## This session's investigation: reform-applied pipeline end-to-end + ambiguity comparison

User regenerated `LexiqueMixte.tsv` with all six reform flags on and ran `dictionary.py`
successfully (no crash) — confirming the fix from commit `26be94d` plus everything else holds up
under the full reform-applied dataset, not just the traditional-spelling default.

Ran the standard verification comparison (`python -m src.ambiguitychecker`, fresh pickles both
sides) between traditional spelling and full-reform-applied:

| Metric | Traditional (baseline) | Reform-applied | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-word clusters | 51087 | 51084 | -3 |
| Same-lemma ambiguous clusters | 49298 | 49306 | +8 |
| **Lemma-homophone ambiguous clusters** | **7391** | **7378** | **-13 (improvement)** |
| Cross-category clashes (aller-style) | 99 | 100 | +1 |
| Overflow frequency mass (n>=5 lemmas) | 110173.62 (18.90%) | 110173.62 (18.90%) | **exactly flat** |

The overflow-mass being *exactly* flat despite the 5-lemma/6-lemma bucket counts shifting
(39->40, 14->13) is the same phenomenon already documented in `STATUS.md` from an earlier,
narrower version of this same comparison (Phase 1, LEMMES+ORTHO only): a merged pair's new
spelling coincidentally unions two previously-separate overflow clusters, moving frequency mass
between overflow buckets without changing the total. Not a bug — an expected, previously-seen
side effect of collapsing spelling variants.

**Net read**: applying the full 1990 reform is a mild, real improvement on the primary metric
(13 fewer lemma-homophone ambiguity clusters) with no change to the overflow-mass metric that
actually drives the special-key budget problem, and a negligible (+1) cross-category-clash
uptick. Nothing alarming; nothing that changes any earlier phase's conclusions.
